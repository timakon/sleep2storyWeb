# German and Dutch search wording

Research date: 7 September 2026. Three targeted formulations per language were checked with live web search. The searches inform wording and intent; they do not establish search volume, rankings, keyword difficulty or a quantified content gap. Results were mixed and sometimes matched a page's read-aloud control rather than its subject. No demand figures are inferred.

## German

- Primary phrase: **zu müde zum Vorlesen**.
- Supporting phrases: **keine Kraft für eine Gute-Nacht-Geschichte**; **Vorlesen bei Halsschmerzen**; **heisere Stimme schonen**; **Hörgeschichte statt Vorlesen**.
- Title: **Zu müde zum Vorlesen? Ideen für heute Abend | Sleep2Story**.
- H1: **Zu müde zum Vorlesen?**
- Intent: an exhausted parent needs a manageable choice for tonight. The article answers that first, then covers a sore voice and preparation for another evening. Formal **Sie/Ihr** matches the existing German guides; familiar dialogue with the child uses **du**.

Queries actually run:

1. `"zu müde" "vorlesen" Eltern Gute Nacht`
2. `"keine Kraft" "Gute-Nacht-Geschichte"`
3. `vorlesen heiser Halsschmerzen Stimme schonen`

Observed evidence:

- [Parents discussing reading aloud or audiobooks](https://www.reddit.com/r/Eltern/comments/1faaiza) uses tiredness, reading length and the choice of a reader or recording in everyday language. This is evidence of parent phrasing, not proof of a developmental or sleep benefit.
- [University Hospital Tübingen: Was hilft bei Heiserkeit?](https://www.medizin.uni-tuebingen.de/puls-online/articles/wenn-die-stimme-wegbleibt) supports **Heiserkeit** and **Stimme schonen** as natural local voice-care terminology.
- [gesund.bund.de: Kehlkopfentzündung](https://gesund.bund.de/kehlkopfentzuendung-laryngitis) also surfaced, but **Vorlesen** is an accessibility control on the result. It is not evidence of a parenting article or demand for reading while ill.

The exact “keine Kraft” search also returned fictional stories where a character lacks strength. It therefore provides weak evidence for the exact parent query. The primary phrase and its supporting variants are editorial choices grounded in the task and natural German, not measured winning keywords. **Gute-Nacht-Geschichte** remains in the description and body to make the bedtime context clear without lengthening every heading.

## Dutch

- Primary phrase: **te moe om voor te lezen**.
- Supporting phrases: **geen puf om voor te lezen**; **voorlezen met keelpijn**; **verhaaltje voor het slapengaan**; **luisterverhaal voor kinderen**.
- Title: **Te moe om voor te lezen? Dit kan vanavond | Sleep2Story**.
- H1: **Te moe om voor te lezen?**
- Intent: practical choices when a parent has no energy or a sore throat. Use **je/jij**, **geen puf**, **plaatjes kijken** and **welterusten**, consistent with the existing approachable Dutch guides. **Keelpijn** names the voice-related situation without turning the page into medical advice.

Queries actually run:

1. `"te moe" "voor te lezen"`
2. `"keelpijn" "voorlezen"`
3. `voorlezen voor het slapengaan moe ouders luisterverhaal`

Observed evidence:

- [Max de Muis, publisher description on Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/be/podcast/max-de-muis-voorleespodcast/id1580438680) directly addresses parents with little energy or inspiration and pairs that situation with a bedtime audio story. This supports the informal **geen puf** wording; its promotional claims are not adopted.
- [Stichting Lezen: Vroeg begonnen, veel gewonnen](https://www.lezen.nl/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Vroeg_begonnen_veel_gewonnen_2022_digitaal.pdf) uses language around **voorlezen**, books and routines at **slapengaan**. The result confirms local subject vocabulary; no study findings or statistics are added to the article.
- [Thuisarts: Keelpijn](https://www.thuisarts.nl/keelpijn) surfaced for the combined sore-throat/reading query, partly because it has a **Voorlezen** accessibility control. This is a false thematic match, not evidence that reading with a sore throat is a high-demand keyword.

The exact fatigue query gave limited direct parent-guide matches. The primary phrase is a natural intent-led editorial choice. The new title and H1 use everyday **voorlezen**, while the metadata and prose carry the more explicit **verhaaltje voor het slapengaan**.

## Shared factual boundaries

[NIDCD: Taking Care of Your Voice](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/taking-care-your-voice) was opened and checked. Its voice-care section advises resting a hoarse or tired voice and avoiding whispering as well as other vocal extremes. Both localizations link to this English source with a localized **English** label. The AAP and WIPO source labels also explicitly identify English-language destinations.

Both pages distinguish tonight's existing options from later preparation. Sleep2Story remains in preparation for iOS and Android, with English and Russian narration planned for launch. A localized website does not imply narration in that language. The product passage requires the complete story text and a voice sample used with consent, describes synthetic narration, and adds no download, export, offline, treatment or sleep-benefit promise. Playback and internet checks concern the reader's chosen player, not an unverified Sleep2Story feature.
