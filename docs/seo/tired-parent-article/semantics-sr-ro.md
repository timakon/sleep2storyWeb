# Serbian and Romanian semantic notes

Research date: 7 September 2026. Scope: the complete tired-parent article in Serbian Latin (`sr`) and Romanian (`ro`). Existing localized guides supplied the site vocabulary and tone. The cached site metadata dated 6 September 2026 identifies Sleep2Story as a prelaunch app and distinguishes website languages from planned English/Russian narration; live searches below were used for wording evidence.

## Serbian Latin

Primary phrase: **preumorni za priču za laku noć**. This is an editorial long-tail formulation, used in the title to express the immediate problem. It is not a measured keyword.

Related phrases:

- **priče za laku noć** — established topic wording, consistent with existing Serbian pages.
- **audio-priča za laku noć** — the already-chosen listening option, not a promise that the app is available.
- **boli me grlo, a dete želi da mu čitam** — spoken question intent reflected naturally in the FAQ.
- **snimanje priče telefonom** — preparation for another day and the related recording guide.

Exact live query formulations:

1. `umorni roditelji priče za laku noć čitanje`
2. `boli me grlo čitanje detetu priče audio`
3. `site.rs "audio priče" "decu"`

Evidence inspected:

- [Humana Serbia: Priče za laku noć](https://humana-baby.rs/vodic-za-roditelje/price-za-laku-noc) uses the central bedtime-story phrase in a Serbian parent guide. Used as evidence of wording only; its sleep claims were not adopted.
- [Radio-televizija Vojvodine: Audio-priče za laku noć na mobilnoj aplikaciji „Pričaj mi”](https://rtv.rs/sr_lat/vojvodina/novi-sad/audio-price-za-laku-noc-na-mobilnoj-aplikaciji-pricaj-mi_1580808.html) supports the local audio-story vocabulary in the search result. It does not establish anything about Sleep2Story.
- [Novosadska novinarska škola: Kovčežić](https://novinarska-skola.org.rs/kovcezic-audio-pripovetke-na-jezicima-nacionalnih-manjina-2018/) is a project owner's account using “audio priča za decu” and referring to Serbian-language audio stories; inspected as a search result.

Localization choices: Serbian Latin throughout, ekavian forms (`dete`, `deca`, `veče`) and the site's plural/polite address. `Nemam snage` and `govor stvara tegobe` give the tired-parent and voice-discomfort passages natural spoken wording. `Sintetička naracija` is explained as a generated result rather than an original reading.

Limitations: broad searches mixed Serbian, Croatian and other regional results. The sore-throat/story combination did not yield a specific, dependable Serbian content cluster. Regional results and fiction excerpts were not treated as Serbian demand evidence. No country-pinned SERP, Search Console data, search volumes, ranking positions or difficulty metrics were available; none are claimed.

## Romanian

Primary phrase: **povestea de seară când nu mai ai energie**. The title and opening use the natural question “Nu mai ai energie pentru povestea de seară?” rather than a word-for-word exhaustion label. This is an editorial long tail, not a measured keyword.

Related phrases:

- **povești de seară** — the established topic and the site's existing terminology.
- **poveste audio de seară** — the practical listening alternative and FAQ intent.
- **mă doare gâtul, dar copilul vrea să îi citesc** — conversational problem wording in the FAQ.
- **înregistrarea unei povești cu telefonul** — preparation intent and the recording-guide link.

Exact live query formulations:

1. `părinți obosiți povestea de seară citit`
2. `mă doare gâtul povești audio copii seara`
3. `"părinți obosiți" "povești"`

Evidence inspected:

- [Clubul Bebelușilor: Povestea de seară înainte de culcare](https://clubulbebelusilor.ro/importanta-povestii-de-seara/) uses the central evening-story phrase in a parent-facing article. It supports terminology, not equivalent benefits between shared reading and audio.
- [Somnoroși: Povești audio pentru copii](https://somnorosi.ro/) is an audio-story publisher using the local listening vocabulary; the homepage was opened.
- [Asociația Spune-mi o Poveste: Ghidul povestitorului](https://spuneopoveste.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/sops-ghidul-povestitorului-iunie.pdf) appeared in the exact tired-parent search with guidance mentioning both children and parents being tired. Only the search extract was available: opening the PDF returned an error, so no broader claim about its contents is made.

Localization choices: Romanian diacritics throughout and the existing site's direct singular address. “Nu mai ai energie” avoids an awkward literal headline and fits ordinary speech. The reader's hoarseness and discomfort are addressed as reasons to rest the voice, with `repaus vocal` explained through everyday options. Product copy uses `narațiune sintetică`, explicitly distinct from the original reading.

Limitations: the exact tired-parent phrase returned a mix of reading guidance, commercial pages and unrelated results. The sore-throat/story combination did not demonstrate a dedicated Romanian demand cluster. No search-volume, country-specific rank, conversion or difficulty data were obtained; the chosen long tails are editorial judgments.
